Industrial Data Valuation

How can a portfolio of industrial datasets be valued as an intangible asset in a way that is transparent, reproducible and honest about its uncertainty?

All values are relative. They rank datasets against each other to decide where to act first; they are not an opposable price.

Limits first

  • The case is anonymised and every amount is a working assumption, consistent with the others, not accounting data.
  • The income approach carries most of the value, so results depend on assumptions about future benefits.
  • Compliance weights are set by judgement; pairwise comparisons (AHP) are used to test them, with an illustrative matrix.
  • A high compliance score shows that data is ready to become an asset, not that it has become one: rights registration and balance-sheet recognition are outside the grid.

Approach

1 · Compliance
Eight datasets scored on 17 criteria in three blocks: documentation, intrinsic quality, fitness for the model.
2 · Valuation
Cost, market and income approaches, each adjusted by the compliance score, blended by confidence.
3 · Uncertainty and priorities
Sensitivity analysis, 10,000-draw Monte Carlo simulation, AHP weighting, and a value × exposure matrix that sorts datasets into four priority groups.
V = QRU × Σ(Val × ICF) / Vp

Reference formula of the DATAMITE data valuation framework: a quality, relevance or utility score (QRU) times the potential value of each component (Val) weighted by its information cost fraction (ICF), corrected for processing time (Vp).

The method sorts value rather than inflating it: well-documented datasets gain, the others lose, and the portfolio barely moves. The most profitable improvements are scale completeness and annotation quality on the datasets closest to the threshold.
